About NSC Calculator

The NSC calculator estimates the maturity value of a five-year National Savings Certificate using annual compounding. The National Savings Institute currently lists the five-year NSC rate at 7.7% in its 2026–27 quarterly table.

NSC is a fixed-tenure small-savings product, so this calculator is intentionally simple: enter the investment and rate, then review the estimated interest and maturity value. It does not try to model every tax situation or premature-closure rule.

How to use NSC Calculator

  1. Enter the certificate amount you want to model.
  2. Review the default rate and change it only when you want to model another notified rate or scenario.
  3. Keep the tenure at five years for the standard NSC estimate.
  4. Select Calculate and compare the investment, estimated interest and maturity value.
  5. Use the result as a planning estimate and verify current product rules before using the estimate.

Technical limitations

  • The standard calculation shown here uses a five-year tenure and annual compounding.
  • It does not calculate personalised tax liability or confirm eligibility for any deduction.
  • Premature closure, pledge, nomination and other account rules are outside this estimate.
  • Government-notified rates can change for new investment periods; the displayed default should be checked against the current official rate table.

Frequently asked

What rate is used by default for NSC?

The calculator starts at 7.7%, matching the National Savings Institute rate table currently shown for the five-year NSC.

Why is the NSC tenure fixed at five years?

The calculator is designed around the current five-year National Savings Certificate product, so the standard estimate does not invite arbitrary tenures.

Does the calculator include tax treatment?

No. Tax treatment depends on the applicable law, your circumstances and the relevant conditions. The calculator focuses on the mathematical maturity estimate.

Is the maturity amount guaranteed?

The estimate follows the selected rate and compounding assumption. Confirm the applicable notified rate and product terms for the certificate you actually purchase.
