Collage Maker Guide

Choose a useful grid, spacing and image order for a clean collage.

A useful collage is more than placing photographs into boxes. Layout, crop, spacing and output quality all affect whether the final image feels balanced, readable and easy to share.

Choose a layout for the story

Start with the number of photographs and the shape of the final image. A small collection can work well as a simple grid, while a larger set benefits from an arrangement that gives the most important photographs enough space.

Control crop and spacing

Equal gaps make a collage feel organised, but excessive spacing can separate photographs that belong together. Check every crop before exporting so faces, documents and important objects are not cut at awkward points.

Use advanced controls deliberately

Frame width, JPEG quality and shuffle are useful because they affect both appearance and file size. Shuffle can help compare arrangements quickly; quality should be chosen according to whether the result is for messaging, a website or printing.

Review the exported image

Open the downloaded collage and check small faces, text inside photographs and the outer edges. A large editor preview can hide problems that become obvious after compression or on a phone.

Frequently asked questions

What should I check before downloading?

Check the visible result, important input values and the exported file itself. A successful action does not guarantee that the final file is correct.

Can I use the result for an official submission?

Only after checking the receiving organisation’s current requirements. These tools are preparation and calculation aids, not official certification services.

What should I do with the original file?

Keep the original until you have opened and checked the exported copy. That makes corrections much easier if a setting was wrong.

Practical tip: Keep the original input until the exported result has been opened and checked.

Plan the final use

A collage for a WhatsApp message can be smaller and more compressed than one intended for printing. Decide the destination before choosing output quality. If the image will be viewed mostly on phones, prioritise readable faces and a manageable file size. If it will be printed, preserve more detail and check the final dimensions.

Avoid the crowded-grid problem

More photographs do not always make a better collage. When every image is given equal space, the viewer may not know where to look first. Remove weak or repetitive photographs and let the stronger images carry the story. A simple layout with fewer good photographs often feels more intentional than a dense grid.

Keep text inside photos readable

If your collage contains certificates, invitations or screenshots, crop them so the important text remains visible. JPEG compression can make small text harder to read, especially after the collage is resized again by a messaging application. Open the final exported image at its actual size before sharing.

Use shuffle as a comparison tool

Shuffle is most useful when you already know which photographs belong in the collage. Generate a few arrangements, compare the balance of faces and colours, and then stop once the composition is clear. Avoid changing the layout repeatedly without checking the full image because a visually attractive local arrangement can create an awkward overall sequence.

Common collage mistakes

The biggest problems are usually not technical. Too many images can make every photograph compete for attention, while inconsistent crops can make a clean grid feel accidental. Another common mistake is exporting a very large image and letting a messaging service compress it again. Decide the intended use first, then choose a sensible output size and quality.

Before you finish

View the completed collage at the size a recipient will actually see. Check faces, small text, borders and the outer frame. If the collage is for printing, also inspect it at a larger size and avoid relying on a phone preview alone. Keep the source photographs separately so you can replace one weak image without rebuilding the whole composition.

A simple editing sequence

A reliable sequence is: select the photographs, remove obvious duplicates, choose a layout, inspect the crop, adjust the frame and spacing, then choose output quality. Do not compress at the beginning because you may need to re-edit the composition. Likewise, do not judge the final quality from the editor alone. The exported image is what a recipient will see, and another service may compress it again after upload.
